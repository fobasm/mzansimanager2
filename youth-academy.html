The Youth Academy Guide

The youth academy is the cheapest way to build a strong, lasting squad. A club that develops its own players spends less on wages and transfers and has valuable players to sell when it needs funds.

How the academy works

Your academy produces young prospects. You can upgrade the academy to raise the quality of the prospects it generates, and you decide which of them to promote into the senior squad. Prospects start with modest ratings but carry a potential rating that shows how good they might become. The higher the potential, the more valuable the player can be in the long run.

Who to promote

  • Choose players whose potential is well above your current starters, not just those with the best rating today.
  • Promote prospects in positions where you are thin.
  • Give young players game time. Players who sit on the bench can become unhappy, so make sure promoted youngsters have a path to playing time.

The financial case

Academy players are cheap to keep and have no transfer fee. A promoted youngster who becomes a starter saves you the cost of a signing, and one who becomes a star can be sold for a large fee. In the lower divisions, where budgets are small, this is often the difference between standing still and rising through the pyramid.

Patience pays

Young players take time to grow. Expect a prospect to need a few seasons before reaching his best. A club that promotes two or three players every season will have a steady pipeline of talent within a few years, and the squad stays young, cheap and motivated.

Common mistakes

  • Promoting players before they are ready and leaving them on the bench.
  • Ignoring the academy when finances are tight.
  • Selling every talented youngster instead of keeping the best.
