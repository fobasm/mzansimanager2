The Transfer Market Guide

The transfer market is where a good manager separates themselves from an average one. This guide explains how the windows work and how to get the most from your budget.

Transfer windows

Transfers follow the real football calendar. The summer window runs through July and August, and the January window runs for the whole month. The Transfers page and the top bar always show how many days remain, so you never miss a deadline. Outside the windows you can still negotiate and plan, so use that time to build a shortlist.

Bidding for players

You can bid on any listed player. Whether a bid is accepted depends on the player's value, which reflects his rating, age and potential. Young players with high potential cost far more than their current rating suggests, and older players are usually cheaper. If a bid is turned down, adjust the fee or look at alternatives instead of repeating the same offer.

Free agents

Free agents cost no transfer fee, only wages. They are the best way to strengthen a squad on a small budget, especially in the lower divisions. Check the free agents list at the start of every window, because the better ones are signed quickly.

Selling and releasing

Selling players you do not need raises money and cuts your wage bill. Releasing a player on a long contract can be costly, so selling is almost always better. Use the money to fix weaker positions instead of adding more of what you already have.

Contracts

Every player has a contract that runs out on a set date. A player who reaches the end of his deal leaves for nothing, so renew your key players before that happens. Players who are unhappy, for example because they are not getting games, can be harder to keep, so keep an eye on morale.

Five rules for a better squad

  • Fix your weakest starting position first.
  • Pay for potential only if you can wait for it.
  • Do not let your best players enter the final year of a contract.
  • Keep some budget back for January.
  • Check the wage bill before every signing, not just the fee.

Plan before the window opens

The best transfer work happens before the window opens. Use the closed period to list your weakest starting positions, check which contracts end soon, and build a shortlist of players you could afford. When the window opens, you can act quickly instead of searching while the deadline approaches.

How to judge a player's value

A player's price reflects his rating, age and potential. A 19-year-old with high potential costs far more than a 30-year-old with the same rating, because he will improve. If you need results this season, buy the older player. If you are building for the future and can wait, pay for potential. Decide which you need before you look at prices.

A simple example

Imagine your left-back and your striker are the two weakest players in your lineup, and you have a limited budget. Check the free agents first: a free agent costs no fee, so your whole budget can go to the position that free agents cannot fill. Before you bid, check the wage bill. A cheap fee with a high wage costs you every month. If a bid is turned down, adjust the fee or look at alternatives instead of repeating the same offer.

Common transfer mistakes

  • Buying more of what you already have. Fix weak positions before adding depth.
  • Ignoring wages. A signing that breaks your wage bill hurts your finances all season.
  • Letting key players run down their contracts. They leave for nothing after the final year.
  • Spending the whole budget in the summer. Keep something back for January.
  • Releasing players on long contracts. Selling is usually cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

When can I sign players?

The summer window runs through July and August, and the January window runs for the whole month. The top bar always shows how many days remain.

Are free agents any good?

Some are. They cost no fee, only wages, which makes them the best way to strengthen a small-budget squad. The better ones are signed quickly, so check the list at the start of each window.

Why was my bid turned down?

The offer was probably below the player's value. Raise the fee or choose someone else.

Should I sell or release a player I do not need?

Sell if you can. It raises money and cuts your wage bill.
