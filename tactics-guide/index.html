The Tactics Guide

Tactics are how you turn a group of players into a team. Mzansi Manager gives you a choice of formations, three team-wide settings and individual roles. Here is how to use each of them.

Formations

The game offers ten formations: 4-4-2, 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1, 4-1-2-1-2, 3-5-2, 5-3-2, 4-3-1-2, 3-4-3, 3-4-1-2 and 5-4-1. Back-four formations such as 4-4-2 and 4-3-3 are the safest starting point. Three-at-the-back systems commit more players to midfield and attack, while 5-3-2 and 5-4-1 protect a lead or help a weaker side hold out. The best formation is the one that fits the players you actually have, so look at your squad before you choose.

Mentality

  • Defensive: fewer chances at both ends. This helps the underdog stay in the game.
  • Balanced: no shift in attack or defence.
  • Attacking: more goals at both ends. This pays off for the stronger side.

Pressing

  • Low block: sit deep and stay compact, with fewer cards.
  • Mid block: the standard shape.
  • High press: wins the ball high up the pitch and works best against weaker sides, but the players tire after about 60 minutes and cards become more likely.

Tempo

Slow tempo means fewer goals in the match, while fast tempo means more. Pace is shared between the two teams, so tempo only matters if you and your opponent play differently. A fast tempo suits a stronger team that wants goals, and a slow tempo suits a side that wants to keep the game under control.

Player roles

  • Full-backs: Overlap (more attack, less cover) or Hold (more cover, less attack).
  • Midfielders: Playmaker (more attack, creates many more assists) or Destroyer (breaks up play and protects the defence).
  • Forwards: Poacher (lives in the box and scores more but does no defending) or Presser (leads the press, with more cover and less attack).

Putting it together

Against a weaker team at home, try an attacking mentality with a high press and fast tempo. Away to the strongest side in the division, a defensive mentality with a low block can earn a valuable point. Always check player stamina before a match, because tired players will undo any plan. At half-time you can change your approach and make up to five substitutions, so use the interval to respond to what you are seeing on the pitch.

Worked examples

The stronger side at home

Your squad is rated well above the visitors. Use a back four such as 4-3-3 or 4-2-3-1 with an attacking mentality, a high press and a fast tempo. The aim is to score early and stay in control. Remember that a high press tires players after about 60 minutes and makes cards more likely, so plan your substitutions around the hour mark. Once you lead, consider dropping to a mid block to save energy.

The underdog away from home

If you are facing the strongest side in the division, switch to a defensive mentality with a low block and a slow tempo. A five-man defence such as 5-4-1 gives extra cover. Pick a Destroyer in midfield and keep your full-backs on Hold. A single point away from home is a good result for a weaker team.

Protecting a lead

With a lead late in the match, move to a defensive mentality or a formation with five defenders such as 5-3-2 or 5-4-1, and bring on fresh legs. Keep the tempo slow to reduce the chances for both teams.

Chasing a result

If you are behind at half-time, move to an attacking mentality, speed up the tempo and use a Playmaker or overlapping full-backs. Expect more chances at both ends, because attacking mentality increases goals for and against. Use some of your five substitutions to bring in fresh attackers.

Common tactical mistakes

  • Pressing high in every match. Tired players undo the benefit, and more cards follow.
  • Ignoring stamina. Always check it before the match, not only at half-time.
  • Choosing a formation because it looks good. Pick the one that suits the players you have.
  • Changing everything at once. Adjust one setting at a time, so you can see what helped.
  • Using an attacking mentality against a much stronger side. You will concede more goals.

Frequently asked questions

Which formation should a beginner use?

A back-four formation such as 4-4-2 or 4-3-3 is the safest starting point. Once you understand your squad, you can try three-at-the-back systems.

Does tempo matter if both teams play at the same pace?

Not much. Pace is shared between the two teams, so tempo only changes the match when you and your opponent play differently.

How many substitutions can I make?

You can make up to five substitutions. Use the half-time break to respond to what you are seeing.

When should I change tactics during a match?

Change them at half-time, or when the match situation changes: you take the lead, concede a goal or players tire.
