Mzansi Premier League

The Mzansi Premier League is the top of the pyramid and the division every manager in the game is chasing. Twenty clubs play each other home and away, the champions are crowned in the final round, and the best sides qualify for continental football and the biggest cup competitions.

What it is like to manage here

Managing in the Premier League is about margins. Squads are deeper and stronger than anywhere else, rivals punish mistakes quickly, and a single poor run can drag a club from the title race into a relegation scrap. The board expects results, reputation moves fast in both directions, and the transfer market is where the biggest swings happen. Rotation matters because the season is long and the cups and African fixtures add matches on top of the league programme.

Promotion and relegation

There is no division above, so the aim is the title, continental qualification and staying ahead of the relegation places. The bottom two clubs are relegated to the Mzansi Championship. Every division has twenty clubs and a 38-match league season, with cups and other competitions played around it.

Local rivalries

Several places have more than one club in this division, which creates local rivalries:

  • Durban: Amazulu United, Lamontville FC, Durban FC, Milford United
  • Polokwane: FC Sekhukhune, Polokwane FC, Marumo FC
  • Soweto: Pirates FC, Chiefs FC
  • Mbombela: Witbank FC, Bushbuckridge FC

Manager tips for the Premier League

  • Keep a balanced squad with two good players for every position, because injuries and cup games will test your depth.
  • Use tactics to suit the opponent. Against weaker sides a higher press and a more attacking approach pay off, while away at the strongest clubs a compact shape protects you.
  • Do not overspend on wages. Premier League contracts are expensive, and a bloated wage bill limits what you can do in the next window.
  • Plan for African football. Qualifying for the CAF Champions League brings prestige and prize money but also extra fixtures.
