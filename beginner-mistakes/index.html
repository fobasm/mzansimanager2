10 Beginner Mistakes in Mzansi Manager

Everyone makes mistakes in their first few seasons. Here are the ten that new managers make most often, with an explanation of why each one hurts and how to avoid it.

1. Spending the whole budget in July

It is tempting to use every rand as soon as the summer window opens. But injuries, form and contract problems will create new needs by January. Keep a portion in reserve so that you can respond. A manager who has money in January can fix a problem, while one who has none must live with it.

2. Ignoring the wage bill

A transfer fee is paid once, but a wage is paid every month. Players on high salaries who do not play are the most expensive mistake. Check the wage bill before every signing and every contract renewal, and sell unused players who earn a lot.

3. Buying a star instead of fixing the weak spot

A team is only as strong as its weakest starting position. A brilliant striker will not help if your goalkeeper and defence are poor. Find the lowest-rated starter in your eleven and improve that position first.

4. Playing the same eleven every week

Tired players perform below their ratings and are more likely to be injured. Rotate before cup games and in busy periods. A fresh substitute is often better than an exhausted starter. See the squad rotation guide.

5. Letting contracts run out

When a contract ends, the player leaves for nothing. Renew key players well in advance, or sell them while they still have value. This is especially important for young players with high potential, who are worth much more than their current rating suggests.

6. Using one tactic for every opponent

An attacking approach with a high press works against weaker sides but can backfire against the best teams, where a compact shape is safer. The tactics guide explains mentality, pressing and tempo. Adapt your approach to the opponent and the venue.

7. Neglecting the academy

Many new managers see the academy as optional, but it is the cheapest source of talent in the game. A club that promotes two or three young players each season will have a younger, cheaper and more settled squad in a few years.

8. Forgetting about suspensions

Red cards and an accumulation of yellow cards lead to bans. Before each game, check whether any of your players is close to a suspension. Starting a player who is on the brink of a ban in a game that does not matter is a risk you do not need to take.

9. Panicking after a bad run

A few poor results do not mean your whole approach is wrong. Look for the cause: injuries, tired players, a weak position or a tactical problem. Make one or two targeted changes rather than rebuilding everything. Large changes in the middle of a season upset the balance of the team.

10. Ignoring the board and your reputation

The board sets targets and judges you on them. If you aim well above your squad's ability, you risk failing the target. If you exceed expectations, your reputation rises and bigger clubs notice. Check the board's expectations early in the season and plan with them in mind.

Bonus: not saving a backup plan

The game saves automatically after every matchday in your browser, with three save slots. Because saves live in your browser, clearing your browser data will delete them. If you have a career you care about, avoid clearing site data on that device, and use a separate slot when you want to try a different approach.

A quick pre-season checklist

  • Squad audit: weakest position, ages and contracts.
  • Budget: split between summer and January.
  • Sponsor: upfront or monthly, to match your plans.
  • Tactics: formation and settings that fit your players.
  • Academy: who to promote and who to keep developing.
