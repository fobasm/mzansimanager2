Your Season Review Explained

At the end of every season, Mzansi Manager gives you a review of the year. It is more than a summary: it shows what went well, what did not and what you should plan for next. This guide explains each part of it.

The final table

The review starts with where your club finished, the points you collected and the financial result of the season. Teams are ranked on points, then on goal difference and then on goals scored. If two clubs are still level, they are ordered by name. The top two clubs in each division are promoted, and the bottom two are relegated. In some divisions, the clubs that finish just outside the promotion places take part in a playoff for the last spot.

Promotion playoffs

If your club qualifies for the playoffs, you will receive a message from the board. The better-placed teams host the semi-finals, and the winners play in the final. The playoffs happen before the season review is completed, so you will see the outcome reflected in your promotion or relegation news.

Team of the Season

The game picks a Team of the Season for your division. It selects a starting eleven based on the average match ratings of players during the year, and shows the rating next to each player. Having your players in this team is a good sign that your squad is performing, and it is a useful guide for scouting if you are looking for players for next season.

Your season in numbers

The next section is a set of statistics about your own club:

  • Record: wins, draws and defeats, with goals for and against.
  • Best result and worst result: your biggest win and heaviest defeat.
  • Longest unbeaten run: the longest series of matches without losing, with the longest winning streak also shown if it was three or more.
  • Top scorer and most assists: your leading goalscorer and assist provider.
  • Ever-present: the player who made the most appearances.
  • Best average rating: the player with the highest average match rating, among those with enough appearances.

These numbers help you make decisions. If one player scored most of your goals, you may be too dependent on him and should look for a second scorer. If your worst result was a heavy defeat away from home, check whether your away tactics need to be more careful.

Reading the numbers: an example

This is an invented example to show how to use the review, not real data from the game. Suppose your club finished sixth. The record shows plenty of wins at home and very few away. Your top scorer scored far more than anyone else, your ever-present is your goalkeeper, and your worst result was a heavy defeat on the road against a top side.

  • The home and away split suggests your away tactics are too open. Next season, try a balanced mentality or a mid block on the road.
  • The gap between your top scorer and the rest suggests that you rely on one player. A second scorer would reduce the risk if he is injured.
  • An ever-present goalkeeper is a good sign, but it also means you may not have a reliable backup. Check your depth in that position.

The review does not make decisions for you, but it shows where to look.

Mood of the season

The review also gives a short description of your season, from a season to remember to a season of struggle, based on your final league position. It is a small touch, but it helps you judge a season quickly.

Retirements and breakthroughs

Older players retire at the end of the season, and the review lists those from your club, together with their ages and appearances. Young players who have improved a lot may be mentioned as breakthrough players. Use this information to plan your squad for the next season, as retirements can leave gaps that you need to fill in the transfer window.

The board and your reputation

After the season, the board assesses your performance against its target. Your reputation changes depending on how well you did, and it can lead to new job offers from other clubs. You can accept a new job or choose to stay loyal to your club. If you did badly, you may face the sack, and the game will offer you options for finding a new club.

Sponsorship for the new season

After the review, you are offered sponsorship deals for the next season. Each deal lasts one season. Some pay the full amount immediately, while others pay monthly. Choose the one that matches your plans for the transfer window and your need for steady cash flow. The club finances guide explains the difference in detail.

Using the review to plan

  1. Note the positions where you were weak or injured.
  2. List the players who are retiring or whose contracts end.
  3. Check the finances: income, wage bill and net result.
  4. Decide which academy players to promote.
  5. Choose a sponsor and set your budget for the summer window.

Frequently asked questions

When does the season review appear?

It appears at the end of the season, after the league and any playoffs have finished.

What happens if I am sacked?

If the board is unhappy with your results, you can lose your job. The game then offers you options for finding a new club, so your career continues.

Does the review affect my next season?

It does not change the game by itself, but the events it describes do: retirements leave gaps, your reputation changes the offers you receive, and the sponsor you choose shapes your budget. Use it as a planning tool.
