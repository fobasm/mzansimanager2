The Youth Academy Guide

The youth academy is the cheapest way to build a strong, lasting squad. A club that develops its own players spends less on wages and transfers and has valuable players to sell when it needs funds.

How the academy works

Your academy produces young prospects. You can upgrade the academy to raise the quality of the prospects it generates, and you decide which of them to promote into the senior squad. Prospects start with modest ratings but carry a potential rating that shows how good they might become. The higher the potential, the more valuable the player can be in the long run.

Who to promote

  • Choose players whose potential is well above your current starters, not just those with the best rating today.
  • Promote prospects in positions where you are thin.
  • Give young players game time. Players who sit on the bench can become unhappy, so make sure promoted youngsters have a path to playing time.

The financial case

Academy players are cheap to keep and have no transfer fee. A promoted youngster who becomes a starter saves you the cost of a signing, and one who becomes a star can be sold for a large fee. In the lower divisions, where budgets are small, this is often the difference between standing still and rising through the pyramid.

Patience pays

Young players take time to grow. Expect a prospect to need a few seasons before reaching his best. A club that promotes two or three players every season will have a steady pipeline of talent within a few years, and the squad stays young, cheap and motivated.

Common mistakes

  • Promoting players before they are ready and leaving them on the bench.
  • Ignoring the academy when finances are tight.
  • Selling every talented youngster instead of keeping the best.

A worked example

Imagine you manage a club in the lower divisions with a small transfer budget and two weak positions in your lineup. Buying two players would use most of your budget and add to your wage bill. Instead, check your academy prospects. If one has potential well above your current starter in that position, promote him, give him regular games and use your budget on the other gap. Within a season or two the youngster should be a first-team player at almost no cost, and your budget has done twice the work.

Upgrading versus spending

An academy upgrade raises the quality of the prospects the academy produces. It is a long-term investment: the payoff comes over several seasons, not at once. If you plan to stay at a club for many seasons, upgrade early. If the board is demanding results this season and you are close to a bad review, a signing may help more in the short term. A good rule is to do both when you can, and to prefer the academy when your finances are tight.

How to read a prospect

  • Current rating: how good he is today.
  • Potential: how good he might become. This is what decides his long-term value.
  • Position: promote where you are thin, not where you are already strong.
  • Gap to your starters: a prospect close to your starters' level can play soon; one far below needs more time.

Keeping young players happy

Young players want to play. A promoted prospect who sits on the bench for months can become unhappy, which makes him harder to keep. Plan regular game time, such as league matches against weaker teams or cup rotation, so your youngsters keep developing.

When to sell a talented youngster

A high-potential player can bring in a large fee. Sell when you need money for a key signing or to meet your finance objective, and keep the best ones who fit your own lineup. Do not sell every talented youngster, or your academy only funds other clubs.

Frequently asked questions

How many players should I promote each season?

Two or three is a good pace. It keeps a steady pipeline of talent without crowding your squad.

Do academy players cost a transfer fee?

No. Academy players have no transfer fee, and they are cheap to keep.

How long does a prospect take to develop?

Expect a few seasons before he reaches his best.

Is the academy worth it in the top division?

Yes. A young player who becomes a star can be sold for a large fee, or can save you the cost of a signing.
