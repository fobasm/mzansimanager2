The Club Finances Guide

A club that spends more than it earns will eventually run into trouble with the board. This guide explains the main parts of your club's finances and how to keep them healthy.

The main numbers

  • Transfer budget: money available to spend on signings.
  • Wage bill: what you pay your squad. It is the biggest ongoing cost.
  • Income: matchday revenue, sponsorship and prize money.
  • Net result: your season's income minus spending, shown on the season review.

The Club tab shows your budget, wage bill, income and spending, and your history season by season. If funds run short, you can ask the board for extra money, but how much they will grant depends on how happy they are with you.

Transfer budget versus wage bill

New managers often treat these as the same thing, but they behave very differently. The transfer budget is a pot of money that goes down when you buy a player and goes up when you sell one. The wage bill is a running cost. A signing is a one-off payment, but his wage is paid for as long as he is at the club. That is why a cheap player on a big contract can be a worse deal than an expensive player on a modest one.

Sponsors

At the start of each season you are offered a choice of sponsors. Each deal runs for that season only. Some sponsors pay the full fee immediately, which gives you a larger transfer budget at once, and others pay in monthly instalments, which spreads the money across the year. If you want to buy players in the summer window, a sponsor who pays upfront is usually the better choice. If you want steadier cash flow, choose monthly payments.

A worked example

These figures are invented to show the idea, not taken from the game. Imagine two sponsors offering the same total over the season. Sponsor A pays everything at the start. Sponsor B pays one twelfth each month.

  • If your squad has a clear hole in goal and you need a new goalkeeper before the first match, Sponsor A lets you buy him in July. Sponsor B would leave you short until the money trickles in.
  • If your squad is already sound and you worry about a slow start, Sponsor B protects you. You never have a large pot to overspend, and the steady income covers wages month by month.

The total is the same, so the real question is timing: do you need the money now, or do you need a calm year?

Keeping the wage bill under control

Wages are easy to add and hard to remove. Before every signing or contract renewal, check how it changes the monthly bill. Players on large contracts who are not in your starting eleven are the first place to look for savings. If one of them is not in your plans, sell him in a window and use the fee and the saved wage to strengthen a weak position.

Prize money and cups

League position and cup runs bring extra income. A good run in the Thatbank Cup or qualification for African competition can improve a season's finances. See the cups guide for what each competition adds, and remember that extra matches also mean extra wear on your squad.

The board and your reputation

The board sets targets each season and judges you on results and sensible management. Finish well and win trophies and your reputation grows, which can bring job offers from bigger clubs. Finish badly or overspend, and the board's patience can run out.

A simple finance routine

  1. At the start of the season, note your budget and wage bill on the Club tab.
  2. Choose the sponsor that matches your summer plans.
  3. Split your budget: spend part in the summer, keep part for January.
  4. Before each signing or renewal, check how the wage bill changes.
  5. At the end of the season, read the net result on the season review and adjust.

Common mistakes

  • Spending the whole budget in one window.
  • Judging a signing by his fee alone and ignoring his wage.
  • Keeping unused players on large contracts.
  • Choosing a monthly sponsor and then expecting a big summer budget.
  • Asking the board for money when they are unhappy with you.

Frequently asked questions

Can I run out of money?

Your budget can run low. If it does, you can ask the board for extra funds, but they may grant little or nothing if they are unhappy with you. It is safer to plan so that you never need to ask.

Does the sponsor deal last more than one season?

No. Each deal lasts for that season, and you choose again at the start of the next one, so you can change your approach every year.

How do I raise more money?

Sell surplus players, finish higher in the league, go further in the cups and choose the sponsor that suits your needs. Developing academy players also saves money you would have spent on signings.
