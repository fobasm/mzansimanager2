The Tactics Guide

Tactics are how you turn a group of players into a team. Mzansi Manager gives you a choice of formations, three team-wide settings and individual roles. Here is how to use each of them.

Formations

The game offers ten formations: 4-4-2, 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1, 4-1-2-1-2, 3-5-2, 5-3-2, 4-3-1-2, 3-4-3, 3-4-1-2 and 5-4-1. Back-four formations such as 4-4-2 and 4-3-3 are the safest starting point. Three-at-the-back systems commit more players to midfield and attack, while 5-3-2 and 5-4-1 protect a lead or help a weaker side hold out. The best formation is the one that fits the players you actually have, so look at your squad before you choose.

Mentality

  • Defensive: fewer chances at both ends. This helps the underdog stay in the game.
  • Balanced: no shift in attack or defence.
  • Attacking: more goals at both ends. This pays off for the stronger side.

Pressing

  • Low block: sit deep and stay compact, with fewer cards.
  • Mid block: the standard shape.
  • High press: wins the ball high up the pitch and works best against weaker sides, but the players tire after about 60 minutes and cards become more likely.

Tempo

Slow tempo means fewer goals in the match, while fast tempo means more. Pace is shared between the two teams, so tempo only matters if you and your opponent play differently. A fast tempo suits a stronger team that wants goals, and a slow tempo suits a side that wants to keep the game under control.

Player roles

  • Full-backs: Overlap (more attack, less cover) or Hold (more cover, less attack).
  • Midfielders: Playmaker (more attack, creates many more assists) or Destroyer (breaks up play and protects the defence).
  • Forwards: Poacher (lives in the box and scores more but does no defending) or Presser (leads the press, with more cover and less attack).

Putting it together

Against a weaker team at home, try an attacking mentality with a high press and fast tempo. Away to the strongest side in the division, a defensive mentality with a low block can earn a valuable point. Always check player stamina before a match, because tired players will undo any plan. At half-time you can change your approach and make up to five substitutions, so use the interval to respond to what you are seeing on the pitch.
