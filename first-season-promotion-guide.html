How to Win Promotion in Your First Season

Promotion is the main goal for most new managers in Mzansi Manager. With two automatic places in every division, and in some seasons a playoff for one more, it is achievable in your first year if you plan carefully. This guide walks through a season from pre-season to the final matchday.

Step 1: audit your squad before you spend anything

Open the Squad page on your first day and look at the whole group, not only the starting eleven. Write down, in your head or on paper, the best player in each position, the age of each starter and how long every contract has left to run. You are looking for three things: holes in your starting eleven, players who are about to leave for nothing, and surplus players who could be sold.

A common mistake is to buy a star striker when the real problem is a weak goalkeeper or a thin defence. A team is only as strong as its weakest starting position, so fix the lowest rating in your eleven first. A decent goalkeeper and two reliable centre-backs often win more points than an extra forward.

Step 2: choose a sponsor that fits your plan

At the start of the season you choose a sponsor, and the deal lasts for that season only. Some sponsors pay the full fee immediately, which gives you cash to spend in the summer window. Others pay monthly, which gives you steadier income through the year. If your squad needs real strengthening before the first match, take the upfront payment. If the squad is already sound and you want to protect yourself against a slow start, monthly payments are safer.

Step 3: use the transfer window wisely

The summer window runs through July and August, and you also get all of January. Do not spend your whole budget in July. Keep a portion back for January, because injuries and form changes will create new needs by then. Check the free agents list early, since it costs nothing but wages and the best players are signed quickly. Aim to finish the summer window with at least two good options for every position.

Watch your wage bill as closely as your transfer fees. A signing with a modest fee and a large salary can hurt you for years. Before you confirm any deal, check how it changes the total wage bill on the Club page.

Step 4: set tactics that fit your players

Pick a formation that suits the players you have, not the one you like best. If you have two strong strikers, a 4-4-2 will use them. If your strength is in midfield, a 4-3-3 or a 3-5-2 puts more of your best players on the pitch. The game offers ten formations, and the tactics guide explains each setting in detail.

As a rule of thumb for promotion, play to your strength at home and stay solid away. At home against weaker sides, an attacking mentality and a higher press win points. Away from home, especially against the best teams in the division, a balanced or defensive approach avoids heavy defeats. Draws are not failures: a promotion-winning team often takes a point from a difficult away game and wins at home.

Step 5: manage the long season

A season is 38 league matches, plus cup games, so fatigue and injuries are as important as tactics. Check stamina and injuries before every match. Rotate players who are tired, especially if a cup tie comes up between league games. A rested substitute with a slightly lower rating will often outperform a tired starter. Yellow and red cards lead to bans, so keep an eye on players who are close to a suspension and plan around it.

The squad rotation guide covers this in more depth.

Step 6: use the January window

By January you will know what your team really needs. If you have a clear weakness, fix it with a free agent or a targeted signing. Resist the temptation to rebuild the squad in the middle of a promotion race, because new players need time to settle and a large number of changes can upset the balance of the team.

Step 7: the run-in

The last ten matches decide promotion. Check the table before each game, and note where your rivals have difficult fixtures. If you are near the automatic places, play to win, but avoid risky tactical changes that expose a weakness. If you finish just outside the top two, you may still have a chance through the promotion playoffs. The better-placed clubs host the semi-finals, so a strong finish gives you an advantage. Rest your key players in any meaningless late fixtures so that they are fresh for the playoffs.

What the board wants

The board sets a target for the season and will judge you against it. Check it early and be realistic. If the target is mid-table and you aim for promotion, you are taking a risk, but if you succeed your reputation rises quickly and bigger clubs may offer you jobs. If you fall short of an ambitious target, the board's patience can wear thin, so match your ambition to your squad.

A simple checklist

  • Fix your weakest starting position first.
  • Choose the sponsor that fits your summer plans.
  • Keep money back for January.
  • Rotate before cup ties and congested periods.
  • Be solid away from home and bold at home.
  • Rest key players before the playoffs, if you reach them.
